\chapter*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Introduction}

Let $a \in \QQ$ and $f = X^2 + a \in \QQ[X]$, with iterates $f_0 = X$ and
$f_{n+1} = f(f_n) = f_n^2 + a$. The pairs $(n, \alpha)$ with $\alpha \in \Qbar$ a root of $f_n$
form an infinite binary rooted tree $T$, in which $(n + 1, \beta)$ is a child of $(n, \alpha)$ when
$\alpha = f(\beta)$. The absolute Galois group of $\QQ$ acts on $T$ by tree automorphisms, which
gives the \emph{arboreal Galois representation} $\rho_a \colon \Gal(\Qbar/\QQ) \to \operatorname{Aut}(T)$;
see \cite{jones} for a survey. The automorphism group of the tree $T_n$ of the first $n$ levels is
the iterated wreath product $\Ct{n}$, a Sylow $2$-subgroup of the symmetric group on $2^n$
letters, and the image of the induced action on $T_n$ is $\Om_n = \Gal(K_n/\QQ)$. So $\rho_a$ is
surjective if and only if $\Om_n \cong \Ct{n}$ for every $n \ge 1$.

\begin{center}
  \emph{For which $a \in \QQ$ is $\rho_a$ surjective?}
\end{center}

The answer is governed by the sequence $c_1 = -a$, $c_{n+1} = c_n^2 + a$, the orbit of $0$ under
$f$ with the sign of the first term changed. By the criterion of \cite{paper}, $\rho_a$ is
surjective if and only if the family $(c_n)_{n \ge 1}$ is \emph{$2$-independent}: no product of
finitely many, but at least one, of its terms is a square in $\QQ$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}).

The proofs in \cite{paper, li2020, li2021} follow Odoni \cite{odoni} and pass from $(c_n)$ to the
sequence $(b_n)$ obtained by multiplicative Möbius inversion, $c_n = \prod_{d \dvd n} b_d$. For
$a \in \ZZ \setminus \{-1, 0\}$, $(c_n)$ is a strong divisibility sequence, so the $b_n$ are pairwise
coprime integers, and $2$-independence reduces to the statement that no $|b_n|$ with $n \ge 2$ is
a square; this is then shown by congruences. That trades arbitrary finite products for single
terms, at the price of working with the more complicated $b_n$.

Here we work with the $c_n$ directly. A group homomorphism $\chi \colon \QQ^\times \to \{\pm 1\}$
--- a \emph{quadratic character} --- is trivial on squares, so a character with
$\chi(c_n) = -1$ and $\chi(c_m) = 1$ for all $m > n$ shows that no product of terms whose
smallest index is $n$ is a square. If such a character $\phi_n$ exists for every $n \ge 1$,
then $\rho_a$ is surjective (Proposition~\ref{prop:crit}), and conversely
(Theorem~\ref{thm:tfae}). The characters we use are the sign, the parities $\val_p$ of the
$p$-adic valuations, and the characters $\chi_p$, $\chi_4$, $\chi_8$ induced by Dirichlet
characters; their values along $(c_n)$ are periodic or eventually constant for reasons that are
local at a single prime, and the arguments become short computations in $\ZZ/N\ZZ$.

This gives, with no more effort than before, the results of \cite{paper, li2020, li2021} for
integer parameters, and extends them to rational parameters $a = r/s$. The main results are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the criterion: $\rho_a$ is surjective iff $(c_n)_{n \ge 1}$ is $2$-independent iff every
        $\phi_n$ exists (Theorem~\ref{thm:tfae});
  \item the main theorem for rational parameters (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}): for $a = r/s$ outside
        the interval $(-2, \alpha]$, $\alpha \approx -1.5437$, in one of three residue classes
        modulo~$4$ determined by the sign of $a + 2$ and the parities, $\rho_a$ is surjective
        as soon as two of three explicit lists of conditions are met;
  \item two families of rational parameters, $a = -j(j+1)$ and $a = -(\ell^2 + \ell + 1)$, that
        lie outside these residue classes (Theorems~\ref{thm:famA} and~\ref{thm:famLi});
  \item the integer results of \cite{paper}, \cite{li2020} and \cite{li2021} as the case $s = 1$
        (Chapter~\ref{chap:integers}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[Vanishing terms]
  \label{rem:nonzero}
  $c_n = 0$ for some $n \ge 1$ holds if and only if $0$ is periodic under $f$, which for
  $a \in \QQ$ happens exactly for $a \in \{-1, 0\}$. (If $p$ divides the denominator of $a$, then
  $v_p(c_n) = 2^{n-1} v_p(a) < 0$; so $a \in \ZZ$, and for $a \ge 1$ or $a \le -3$ the orbit
  tends to infinity; $a = -2$ gives $0 \mapsto -2 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 2$.) No statement below needs
  this: a character with $\chi(c_n) = -1$ already forces $c_n \ne 0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Primitive prime divisors]
  \label{rem:ppd}
  Ordering by the \emph{largest} index instead, $(c_n)$ is $2$-independent as soon as for every
  $n \ge 1$ some character $\phi'_n$ satisfies $\phi'_n(c_n) = -1$ and $\phi'_n(c_m) = 1$ for
  $1 \le m < n$. For instance $\phi'_n = \val_p$ works when $c_n$ has a primitive prime
  divisor $p$ (one dividing no earlier term) with $v_p(c_n)$ odd. Primitive prime divisors exist
  for all $n \ge 2$ when $a \in \ZZ \setminus \{-2, -1, 0\}$ (Rice \cite{rice},
  Doerksen--Haensch \cite{doerksen-haensch}), and for $a = r/s \notin (-2, -1)$ for every term
  after the first, except the second one when $r + s = 1$ (Krieger \cite{krieger}, Theorem~1.3);
  but the parity of $v_p(c_n)$ is hard to control. For $a = 3$: $c_1 = -3$,
  $c_2 = 3 \cdot 2^2$, $c_3 = 3 \cdot 7^2$, so $c_2 c_3 = 42^2$ is a square and $\rho_3$ is not
  surjective. The characters $\phi_n$ of the forward ordering are the ones we can construct.
\end{remark}

\section{The plan of the formalization}
\label{sec:order}

\emph{Reused material.} The Galois-theoretic part --- the iterates, their splitting fields and
Galois groups, Odoni's embedding $\Om_n \hookrightarrow \Ct{n}$, the degree criterion and
the irreducibility lemma (Chapter~\ref{chap:galois}), with the general theory of
multiquadratic extensions, wreath products and even polynomials it rests on
(Appendix~\ref{chap:auxiliary}) --- exists for rational $a$ and is taken over. It contains
nothing about Möbius inversion. The same holds for the order-theoretic facts about $(c_n)$ for
$-2 \le a \le 0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sumsign}).

\emph{Devices.} Quadratic characters are Mathlib's \lname{MulChar ℚ ℤ} (a commutative group;
the value at $0$ is $0$, so $\chi(c_n) = -1$ carries $c_n \ne 0$ with it), and the characters
induced by Dirichlet characters come from Mathlib's \lname{quadraticChar (ZMod p)},
\lname{ZMod.χ₄} and \lname{ZMod.χ₈} through one construction (Definition~\ref{def:chars}).
The integer sequence $w_n$ (Definition~\ref{def:wseq}) carries the arithmetic of $c_n = r w_n/s^{2^{n-1}}$;
for a modulus $N$ prime to $s$, the identity $r w_n = s^{2^{n-1}} \bar c_n$ in $\ZZ/N\ZZ$, with
$(\bar c_n)$ the sequence of the parameter $\bar a = r/s \bmod N$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}),
turns every local argument into a computation with the sequence $(\bar c_n)$ in $\ZZ/N\ZZ$.

\emph{Order of work.} Each step is a round of declarations written and audited in a scratch
sandbox, then committed through the verification chain.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Galois side: move the reused declarations into the new layout; prove
        Theorems~\ref{thm:criterion} and~\ref{thm:criterion-infinite} (the converse direction
        is new).
  \item Characters: Definition~\ref{def:chars}, Chapter~\ref{chap:characters}, including the
        converse Theorem~\ref{thm:tfae}.
  \item The sequence: Chapter~\ref{chap:sequence}.
  \item Patterns: Chapter~\ref{chap:patterns}.
  \item Criteria and the main theorem: Chapter~\ref{chap:main}.
  \item The families: Chapter~\ref{chap:families}.
  \item Integer parameters: Chapter~\ref{chap:integers}; the comparator challenges for the
        integer results keep their statements and are re-proved by the new theorems, and the
        challenge for the main theorem replaces those of the earlier rational results.
  \item Removal of the earlier route (Möbius factors, the files on Li's results and on rational
        parameters), dead-code and import scans, documentation.
\end{enumerate}
Until step~7 the earlier route stays in the repository, so that every commit keeps all
comparator challenges proved; declaration names that clash with it are resolved by renaming the
old declaration.
