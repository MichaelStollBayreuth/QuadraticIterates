Maximality for x² + a

When is the Galois group of every iterate the full automorphism group of the binary tree? Three papers answer that over ℚ. This library formalizes all three — there is nothing else in the literature to formalize — and adds two theorems of its own, one of them for rational a.

The object

A tower of quadratic extensions, and one criterion

For f(x) = x² + a with a ∈ ℤ, let Kn be the splitting field of the n-th iterate. Galois acts faithfully on the tree of iterated preimages of 0, so Ωn = Gal(Kn/ℚ) embeds in Aut(Tn) ≅ [C₂]n, the n-fold iterated wreath product of the cyclic group C₂ of order 2. The representation is maximal when that embedding is onto, for every n.

Odoni and Stoll reduced maximality to arithmetic. Put c1 = −a and cn = fn(0) for n ≥ 2, and let bn = ∏d|n cdμ(n/d) be the Möbius-inverted sequence, whose terms are pairwise coprime. Maximality at level n is exactly linear independence of the classes of c1, …, cn in the 𝔽₂-vector space ℚ×/(ℚ×)². That equivalence is the spine of the whole subject, and it is the spine of this library:

theorem section1_tfae (ha : ¬IsSquare (-a : ℚ)) (n : ℕ) :
    [Nonempty (GaloisGroup a n ≃* WreathPower n),
     TwoIndependent (fun i : Fin n ↦ (cSeq a ((i : ℕ) + 1) : ℚ)),
     TwoIndependent (fun i : Fin n ↦ (bSeq a ((i : ℕ) + 1) : ℚ))].TFAE

TwoIndependent v is that independence spelled out without quotients: no non-empty subfamily of v has a square product. For non-zero rationals it is exactly 𝔽₂-linear independence of the classes in ℚ×/(ℚ×)², and the library proves the two forms equal.

Because the bn are pairwise coprime, independence follows as soon as no |bn| is a square for n ≥ 2. Everything since 1985 has been a search for values of a where that non-squareness can be forced for all n at once — and the answer depends, remarkably, only on the sign of a and its residue modulo 4.

Two of Stoll's three classes are one theorem, taking the case split as a disjunction of congruences — the shape the paper itself uses:

theorem section3_main
    (hcase : (0 < a ∧ a % 4 = 1) ∨ (0 < a ∧ a % 4 = 2) ∨
             (a < 0 ∧ a % 4 = 0 ∧ ¬IsSquare (-a))) :
    ∀ n ≥ 1, Nonempty (GaloisGroup a n ≃* WreathPower n)

The library also carries more than the headline theorems. Li's Lemma 2.2 — formalized in three parts — handles squarefree n in three of the four unsettled boxes. In every open case the obstruction is the same: non-squarefree n, which is precisely where Li had to retreat to two explicit families.

The frontier

How much is actually left

Running the 2-independence criterion directly over −2000 ≤ a ≤ 2000, to level 8: 1982 of the roughly 4000 admissible values are not settled by any published theorem. No value covered by Stoll or Li shows an obstruction, which is a decent consistency check on the formalized hypotheses.

Within that range there are only two obstructions at all — and both are visible almost immediately:

ValueFails atWhy
a = 3 level 3 Both f²(0) = 12 and f³(0) = 147 carry large square factors, leaving b₂ and b₃ in the same square class.
a = −(m²+1), m odd level 2 b₂ = −a−1 = m² is a positive square.

The second family is the odd twin of Li's index-2 case: his −(4k²+1) is exactly the even-m half. Level 2 by itself is no theorem — with b₁ = −a and b₂ = −a−1 it is an immediate special case of the criterion above, so for every a with −a not a square, Ω₂ is maximal exactly when −a−1 is not a square. What Li proves is far stronger: throughout a ≡ 3 (mod 4), that one level-2 condition already decides every level.

Everything else in the open classes looks maximal and is unproven. Hindes' conjecture would settle it wholesale: if Ω₃ is maximal, so is Ωn for every n.

The search

Nothing has been added since 2021

The citation graph is unusually clear on this point. Li's 2021 paper has no citations at all; his 2020 paper is cited only by his own 2021 sequel. Of the 66 papers citing Stoll 1992 through 2026, not one proves maximality for new integer values of a — they cite it for the criterion, for the post-critically finite classification, for finite-index questions, or for prime-density applications.

Interestingly, there is no way to extend Stoll's method to all other cases where −k is not a square.
Rafe Jones, Galois representations from pre-image trees: an arboreal survey (2013), stating Stoll's three cases as the whole picture

Li reached the same verdict from the other side in 2021: "there seems to be no method other than Stoll's criterion which can show the non-squareness of |bn| for infinitely many n." Five years on, that is still true.

One small exception has since appeared, and it comes out of the formalization itself: Li's family a = −(8k+2)(8k+3) is the even-k half of a = −(4k+2)(4k+3), and the odd half goes through by his own argument once the residue of the rescaled sequence is read modulo 8 rather than modulo 4 — the a ≡ 2 (mod 4) box above. It is a half-family, not a new class: the verdicts of Jones and Li stand.

A second addition leaves the integers. For rational a = r/s the rescaled critical orbit still has integer numerators, and the same reflection argument goes through once r and s are read modulo 4 separately: for a > −1 and for a ≤ −2, in explicit residue classes of (r, s) and under a squarefree-level certificate (a modulus modulo which r, −s or ±s is not a square, or a residue condition modulo 8), every level is maximal — Theorems R⁺ and R⁻ in the table above, Chapter 6 of the blueprint. For s = 1 they reduce to Stoll's three classes and to the sufficient half of Li's dichotomy; the classes with s ≡ 3 (mod 4) are new — 5/3 and 4/3 are maximal, for instance. No integer value of a is added: the lattice above is unchanged.

Adjacent

Where the subject went instead

Work has continued, but along three other axes: moving the root of the tree off the critical point, bounding finitely many levels, and assuming deep conjectures. None of it enlarges the set of a above.

2017

Bush, Hindes & Looper — a family with a shifted basepoint

Surjectivity for φp(x) = (x−p)² + 2p − p², for primes p ≡ 3 (mod 4) and five further congruence classes, plus all p < 5000; and a second family (x−p)² − p² − 1 for p ≡ 2 (mod 5). Completing the square puts these at c = p − p², but the root of the tree moves to −p, so they are not comparable with the results above. Method: Chabauty–Coleman with the Mordell–Weil sieve.

2018

Hindes — maximality up to level five

This does reach into the open classes, but only to finite depth: for c ∈ ℤ ∖ {3} with −c and −(c+1) non-squares, Ω₅ is maximal. Earlier work of his does level 4 and shows that the Hall–Lang conjecture implies finite index; a 2013 paper proves c = 3 is the only integer whose third iterate has a small Galois group. His Conjecture 1.5 — Ω₃ maximal implies Ω∞ maximal — would collapse the whole frontier.

2026

Henderson, Juul, Lattin, Mercado & Schaefer — rational parameters

The one recent paper working over ℚ rather than ℤ: infinitely many surjective examples for c = −a − a² and c = −1 + a − a², with the tree rooted at the strictly preperiodic point a. Rational parameters buy small height — they can land c inside the Mandelbrot set. The rational extension in this library (Theorems R⁺ and R⁻ above) is the complementary case: the tree stays rooted at 0, and the question is which r/s the residue method reaches.

ongoing

Conditional and structural results

Gratton–Nguyen–Tucker (from abc) and Hindes (from Vojta) give finite index, never maximality. Ferraguti, Pagano and Casazza classify which index-two subgroups can occur — Li's C₄[[C₂]n−2] is one of them. Benedetto, Ghioca, Juul and Tucker have now determined the arboreal group for every post-critically finite quadratic, closing off the excluded value a = −2 in both the periodic and the strictly preperiodic case.

The one clean generalization the literature points at is a change of setting, not of parameter: extend the §1 machinery from x² + a rooted at 0 to (x − γ)² + c at an arbitrary basepoint, following Hindes §2, whose Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2 are stated as generalizations of Stoll's Lemmas 1.4–1.6. That single step would bring both of the families above within reach of the existing TwoIndependent API.
